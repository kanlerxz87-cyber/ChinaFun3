Executive summary

  1. Base yourself on the Bund / Huangpu. Huangpu has 12 starred restaurants whose district is confirmed on MICHELIN pages — the densest confirmed cluster, and the Peninsula, Waldorf Astoria and Fairmont Peace are all inside it.
  2. One hotel beats everything on walk-to-dinner. Bvlgari Shanghai houses two starred restaurants in the same building (Bao Li Xuan 2★ and Il Ristorante 1★). The Waldorf Astoria (Ling Long 1★), the Peninsula (Yi Long Court 1★) and the Ritz-Carlton Pudong (Jin Xuan 1★) each house one — confirmed on each restaurant's MICHELIN page.
  3. Only one restaurant price survives verification. Taian Table publishes ¥2,588 / ¥2,888 per person → ¥5,176–5,776 for two before add-ons. Other starred houses do not publish menus that a static page can confirm.
  4. The dates are well chosen. Spring Festival 2027 runs 5–13 February; your window opens the day after it closes, so you avoid peak rates and holiday closures, and still catch the Lantern Festival on 20 February.
  5. Booking timing is evidence-based, not guesswork. Industry 2026 data points to 8–14 days out for city hotels, with the trade-off of losing specific room categories — see Dates & Booking for the exact recommendation and its caveat.
  6. 3 properties are explicitly excluded and why (Four Seasons not yet open in 2027, Ultraviolet closed 2025, one hotel fetched in error).
  7. 51 of 58 restaurants still have no official hours — the single biggest gap in this plan. Every venue that does publish hours (Yu Garden, both Shanghai Museum buildings, Disneyland, 7 starred kitchens) is filled in and linked.
